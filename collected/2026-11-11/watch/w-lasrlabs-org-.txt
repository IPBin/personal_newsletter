About LASR London AI Safety Research Labs is a technical AI Safety research programme focussed on reducing the risk of&nbsp; loss of control to advanced AI. We focus on action-relevant questions tackling concrete threat models. Participants work in teams of three to four, supervised by an experienced AI safety researcher, to take a research project from proposal to publication over 13 weeks. Teams work full-time and in person from the London Initiative for Safe AI , alongside AI safety organisations and researchers. Alumni from previous cohorts have gone on to work at UK AISI, Apollo Research, Geodesic Research, OpenAI, Coefficient Giving and more, while others have founded AI safety organisations or pursued independent research. LASR will also be a good fit for someone hoping to publish: LASR papers have been accepted to top ML venues including NeurIPS, ICLR, and ICML workshops. Recent highlights include three NeurIPS 2025 main conference papers, one selected for an oral presentation, and a best paper award at an ICML 2025 workshop. Programme Details LASR Labs is a 13 week programme which runs thrice a year. Participants receive an £15,000 stipend to cover living expenses in London, and we will also provide food, office space, and travel. In week 0, you will learn about and critically evaluate a handful of technical AI safety research projects with support from LASR. Developing an understanding of which projects might be promising is difficult and often takes many years, but is essential for producing useful AI safety work. Week 0 gives you space to develop your research prioritisation skills and learn about different agendas and their respective routes to value. At the end of the week, you will express preferences about projects, and we will match you into teams. In the remaining 12 weeks, your team will take the project from proposal to publication: designing and running experiments, iterating on results, and writing up an AI safety research paper (as a preprint, workshop paper, or conference paper) with an accompanying blog post. You will meet regularly with your supervisor throughout, and LASR staff support teams on research progress and planning across the programme. Working from the London Initiative for Safe AI means working alongside AI safety researchers and organisations day to day. Throughout the programme we organise talks from leading researchers, a careers fair, and visits to organisations including Google DeepMind and the UK AI Security Institute. Participants regularly attend events at LISA, and past cohorts have found collaborators, mentors, and other opportunities through the space. Topics and supervisors Previous LASR groups have published on important areas in AI safety, focused on reducing risks from advanced AI. We’ve had supervisors from Google DeepMind, UK AISI, Anthropic, and top UK universities. In previous rounds, participants have worked on projects relating to: AI control and untrusted monitoring , data poisoning , interpretability probes , chain-of-thought monitoring and faithfulness , sparse autoencoders , steganographic collusion , and evaluating scheming and evaluation awareness in frontier models. Who should apply? We are looking for applicants with the following skills:&nbsp; Technical ability: Machine learning engineering experience and strong quantitative skills.&nbsp; Research ability: Willingness to experiment, iterate, and dive into execution under uncertainty. An ability to develop a theory of change for a project focussed on impact.&nbsp; Communication skills : An ability to clearly articulate the outcomes and implications of experiments, coupled with transparent reasoning . For more detail on how we think about and measure technical and research ability, refer to “tips for empirical alignment research” by Ethan Perez, which outlines in detail the specific skills valued within an AI safety research environment.&nbsp; Strong participants have looked like: An engineer with several years of industry experience building and shipping production systems, who had been following AI safety and wanted to move into research full-time. Now working at the Australian AI Safety Institute. A chemistry PhD student who had spent years reading and writing about AI safety in his own time, and used LASR to turn that engagement into published technical research. Now working at the Arcadia Alignment Project. An undergraduate who hadn't yet finished his bachelor's, but had founded the AI safety group at his university and built it into a serious community. Now heading to an internship at Redwood Research. There are no specific requirements for experience, but we anticipate successful applicants will have done some of these things: Built something substantial they can point to: a research result, a system or tool, an analysis, an experiment, or a proof, in any field, ML or not. Experience working with LLMs, whether in research, engineering, or serious self-directed projects. Taken real steps toward this kind of work on their own initiative, for example self-teaching ML, switching from another field, or starting a research project independently. Research shows that people from underrepresented groups are more prone to experiencing imposter syndrome and doubting the strength of their candidacy, so we urge you not to exclude yourself prematurely and to submit an application if you're interested in this work.
